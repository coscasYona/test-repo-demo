# Recovery & Injury Prevention Guide

Recovery is where adaptation happens. You don't get fitter during training — you get fitter during recovery from training. With the dual stress of running and lifting in a caloric deficit, recovery becomes even more critical.

## Sleep: The #1 Recovery Tool

Research on athletes consistently shows that sleep is the single most powerful recovery tool (Watson, 2017; Halson, 2014).

| Guideline | Target |
|---|---|
| Duration | 7.5–9 hours per night |
| Consistency | Same bedtime and wake time (±30 min), even weekends |
| Pre-sleep routine | No screens 30 min before bed, cool room (18–20°C) |
| Pre-sleep nutrition | Casein/cottage cheese 30–60 min before bed |

### Sleep Optimization Tips

1. **Blackout curtains** or sleep mask — even small light reduces sleep quality
2. **No caffeine after 2:00 PM** — caffeine half-life is 5–6 hours
3. **Magnesium glycinate (200–400mg)** before bed — supports relaxation and sleep quality
4. **Keep bedroom cool** — 18–20°C is optimal
5. **Consistent wake time** is more important than consistent bedtime

## Mobility & Stretching Routine

Do this routine daily (10–15 minutes). Non-negotiable on rest days and after every run.

### Post-Run Routine (5–8 min)

| Exercise | Duration | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Standing quad stretch | 30 sec/leg | Quadriceps |
| Standing calf stretch (wall) | 30 sec/leg | Calves / Achilles |
| Hip flexor stretch (half-kneeling) | 45 sec/leg | Hip flexors / psoas |
| Pigeon pose or figure-4 stretch | 45 sec/leg | Glutes / piriformis |
| Hamstring stretch (standing or seated) | 30 sec/leg | Hamstrings |
| Thoracic spine rotation | 8 reps/side | Upper back mobility |

### Daily Mobility Routine (10 min — do on rest days or evenings)

| Exercise | Duration/Reps | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Cat-cow | 10 reps | Spine mobility |
| World's greatest stretch | 5 reps/side | Full body opener |
| 90/90 hip switches | 8 reps/side | Hip internal/external rotation |
| Couch stretch | 60 sec/leg | Hip flexors (deep) |
| Ankle circles + dorsiflexion stretch | 10 reps/direction + 30 sec | Ankle mobility |
| Foam roll quads | 60 sec/leg | Quad tissue quality |
| Foam roll IT band area (emphasize glutes/TFL) | 60 sec/leg | Lateral hip |
| Foam roll calves | 60 sec/leg | Calf tissue quality |
| Lacrosse ball on foot arch | 60 sec/foot | Plantar fascia |

## Injury Prevention: The Big 5 Runner Injuries & How to Avoid Them

### 1. Runner's Knee (Patellofemoral Pain)

**Cause:** Weak glutes, overstriding, too much volume too fast
**Prevention:**
- Clamshells: 3 × 15/side (daily)
- Single-leg glute bridges: 3 × 10/side
- Shorten your stride — increase cadence to 170–180 steps/min
- Follow the 10% rule for weekly volume increase

### 2. Shin Splints (Medial Tibial Stress Syndrome)

**Cause:** Running on hard surfaces, worn-out shoes, weak calves
**Prevention:**
- Calf raises: 3 × 15 (daily)
- Toe walks: 2 × 30 sec
- Replace running shoes every 500–800 km
- Run on varied surfaces when possible (trails, grass, track)

### 3. IT Band Syndrome

**Cause:** Weak hip abductors, tight TFL, excessive downhill running
**Prevention:**
- Side-lying leg raises: 3 × 15/side
- Monster walks with band: 2 × 15 steps/direction
- Foam roll glutes and TFL (NOT directly on IT band)
- Avoid excessive cambered road running

### 4. Achilles Tendinopathy

**Cause:** Sudden increases in speed work, tight calves, weak calf muscles
**Prevention:**
- Eccentric heel drops: 3 × 15 (daily) — the gold standard (Alfredson protocol)
- Single-leg calf raises: 3 × 12
- Don't skip the easy/recovery runs — they promote tendon blood flow
- Gradually introduce speed work

### 5. Plantar Fasciitis

**Cause:** Tight calves, weak foot muscles, overtraining
**Prevention:**
- Toe curls on towel: 3 × 15
- Lacrosse ball roll on foot arch: 2 min/day
- Calf stretches: held for 45–60 sec
- Supportive shoes — avoid walking barefoot on hard floors

## When to Back Off

Listen to these warning signs:

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Persistent soreness (> 72 hrs in one area) | Skip the next hard session, do easy run or rest |
| Sharp, localized pain during running | STOP immediately. Rest 2–3 days minimum. See physio if persistent. |
| Resting heart rate elevated > 5 bpm from baseline | Take a rest day — sign of incomplete recovery or illness |
| Poor sleep for 3+ consecutive nights | Reduce training volume by 30% for the week |
| Persistent fatigue despite adequate sleep | Eat at maintenance for 3–5 days (diet break), reduce training |
| Loss of motivation / dread of training | Rest day + fun activity. Reassess weekly volume. |

## Cold/Heat Therapy

| Method | When | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Cold shower or ice bath | After long runs (> 90 min) or hard intervals | 10–15 min at 10–15°C |
| Contrast shower (hot/cold alternating) | Any recovery day | 3 cycles of 2 min hot / 30 sec cold |
| Warm Epsom salt bath | Rest days, before bed | 15–20 min — relaxation + magnesium absorption |

## Mental Recovery

Marathon training is mentally demanding, especially with concurrent strength training and a caloric deficit. Protect your mental health:

1. **One rest day per week minimum** — no running, no gym
2. **One full diet break every 6–8 weeks** — eat at maintenance for 5–7 days (replenishes leptin, reduces cortisol)
3. **Social runs** — join a running group or run with a friend weekly
4. **Process goals** — focus on "I completed my training this week" not just the marathon
5. **Visualization** — spend 5 min before sleep visualizing crossing the finish line
