# The Plan

## Three Rules

1. **Most of your running should feel easy.** If you can't hold a conversation, slow down.
2. **Eat enough protein, eat a bit less overall.** That's the entire diet.
3. **Show up consistently.** A mediocre week you actually do beats a perfect week you skip.

---

## Running

### How You Progress

There are three layers of progressive overload built into this plan:

1. **Duration goes up.** Your long run grows from 35 min to 3 hours. Your easy runs grow from 25 min to 50 min.
2. **Intensity gets added.** Phase 1 is all easy. Phase 2 adds tempo. Phase 3 adds intervals. Phase 4 adds marathon pace work.
3. **Paces get faster naturally.** You don't force this — as your fitness improves, the same effort level produces a faster pace. Re-test every 4 weeks: run 30 min at conversational effort and note your average pace. Update your zones from there.

**The 10% rule:** Total weekly running time never jumps more than ~10% from one week to the next (except recovery weeks, which drop). This is already built into the tables below.

### The Weekly Pattern

Your typical week has 4 runs. The only thing that changes across 20 weeks is how long the long run is and when hard efforts get added.

### The Weekly Pattern

| Day | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tue | Easy run | Conversational pace. ~6:30–7:15/km for you. |
| Thu | Quality run | Easy in Phase 1. Becomes tempo or intervals later. |
| Sat | Long run | The big one. Slow and steady. Walk if you need to. |
| Sun | Short easy run | 20 min. Shake out the legs. Skip if you're wrecked. |

Mon / Wed / Fri are gym or rest (see below).

### Phase 1: Learn to Run More (Weeks 1–4)

You can run 30 min. Let's get you to 60 min.

| Week | Tue (easy) | Thu (easy) | Sat (long) | Sun (shakeout) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 min | 25 min | 35 min | 15 min |
| 2 | 30 min | 25 min | 40 min | 20 min |
| 3 | 30 min | 30 min | 50 min | 20 min |
| 4 | 25 min | 25 min | 35 min | 15 min |

Week 4 is a recovery week. Every 4th week is lighter — this is how you stay healthy.

All runs easy pace. No exceptions.

### Phase 2: Build the Engine (Weeks 5–8)

Introduce one harder effort per week on Thursday.

| Week | Tue (easy) | Thu (quality) | Sat (long) | Sun (shakeout) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 35 min | 10 easy + 15 tempo + 10 easy | 55 min | 20 min |
| 6 | 40 min | 10 easy + 20 tempo + 10 easy | 65 min (~10 km) | 25 min |
| 7 | 40 min | 10 easy + 25 tempo + 10 easy | 75 min (~12 km) | 25 min |
| 8 | 30 min | 10 easy + 15 tempo + 10 easy | 55 min | 20 min |

**Tempo pace:** Comfortably hard. You can say a few words, not sentences. ~5:45–6:15/km.

### Phase 3: Half-Marathon Territory (Weeks 9–12)

Long run breaks 90 min. Add intervals on some Thursdays.

| Week | Tue (easy) | Thu (quality) | Sat (long) | Sun (shakeout) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 45 min | 15 easy + 5×(3 min hard / 2 min jog) + 10 easy | 90 min (~14 km) | 20 min |
| 10 | 45 min | 10 easy + 30 tempo + 10 easy | 100 min (~16 km) | 25 min |
| 11 | 50 min | 15 easy + 6×(3 min hard / 2 min jog) + 10 easy | 110 min (~18 km) | 25 min |
| 12 | 35 min | 10 easy + 20 tempo + 10 easy | 75 min | 20 min |

**Interval "hard" pace:** You can say a word or two, not more. ~5:00–5:30/km.

### Phase 4: Marathon Build (Weeks 13–17)

Long run peaks at ~30 km. Practice eating during long runs (gels, sports drink, whatever you plan to use on race day).

| Week | Tue (easy) | Thu (quality) | Sat (long) | Sun (shakeout) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | 50 min | 15 easy + 25 at marathon pace + 10 easy | 2 hrs (~20 km) | 25 min |
| 14 | 50 min | 15 easy + 5×(4 min hard / 2 min jog) + 10 easy | 2 hr 15 (~22 km) | 25 min |
| 15 | 50 min | 15 easy + 35 at marathon pace + 10 easy | 2 hr 40 (~26 km) | 30 min |
| 16 | 50 min | 15 easy + 30 tempo + 10 easy | 3 hrs (~30 km) | 25 min |
| 17 | 45 min | 15 easy + 40 at marathon pace + 10 easy | 2 hr 10 (~21 km) | 25 min |

**Marathon pace:** ~6:00–6:15/km. This should feel "I could keep going" — not hard.

### Phase 5: Taper & Race (Weeks 18–20)

You've done the work. Now rest up and let your body absorb it.

| Week | Tue (easy) | Thu (quality) | Sat (long) | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | 40 min | 10 easy + 20 marathon pace + 10 easy | 90 min (~14 km) | 20 min |
| 19 | 35 min | 10 easy + 15 marathon pace + 10 easy | 60 min (~10 km) | 20 min |
| 20 | 25 min + strides | 20 min easy | REST | **RACE DAY** |

You'll feel restless during the taper. That's normal. Trust it.

### Race Day Cheat Sheet

- **Km 1–10:** Start 15 sec/km SLOWER than goal pace. You'll thank yourself at km 35.
- **Km 10–30:** Settle into marathon pace. Stay relaxed.
- **Km 30–42:** Break it into 1 km chunks. This is where the long runs pay off.
- **Eat a gel every 30–45 min** starting at km 5. Drink at every aid station. Nothing new — only stuff you practiced.

## Gym

You're already strong. The gym serves two purposes: **grow your bench** (it's lagging) and **keep your squat/deadlift** while running takes over.

### How You Progress in the Gym

One simple rule per lift:

| Lift | Start | Progression Rule | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bench | 37 kg (4×5) | Hit all reps cleanly? Add 2 kg next session. | No ceiling — ride it as long as it goes |
| Squat | 105 kg (4×5) | Hit all reps for 2 weeks straight? Add 2.5 kg. | Cap at 115 kg — don't chase more |
| Deadlift | 145 kg (3×3) | Hit all reps for 2 weeks straight? Add 5 kg. | Cap at 160 kg — don't chase more |
| Everything else | Moderate | Hit top of rep range on all sets? Add smallest increment available. | Don't stress — these are accessories |

**When you stall (and you will):** Drop the weight 10%, build back up. If you stall again on the same lift, hold that weight — it's your maintenance number for the rest of the program.

**Why bench starts at 37 kg, not 45 kg:** You said 45 kg is your fumbled max. Start at ~80% of that. Build with clean reps. You'll blow past 45 kg within a few weeks and actually own the weight this time.

### Weeks 1–8: 3 days/week (Mon, Wed, Fri)

**Day A — Monday (Upper)**

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Starting Weight | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bench Press | 4 × 5 | 37 kg | +2 kg/session when all reps hit |
| Barbell Row | 4 × 6 | 60 kg | +2.5 kg when all reps hit for 2 weeks |
| Overhead Press | 3 × 8 | 25 kg | +1–2 kg when you get 3×8 clean |
| Pull-ups or Lat Pulldown | 3 × 8 | BW or 50 kg | Add 2.5 kg when all reps hit |

**Day B — Wednesday (Lower)**

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Starting Weight | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squat | 4 × 5 | 105 kg | +2.5 kg when all reps hit for 2 consecutive weeks |
| Romanian Deadlift | 3 × 8 | 90 kg | +2.5 kg when all reps hit for 2 weeks |
| Bulgarian Split Squat | 3 × 8/leg | 15 kg DBs | +2 kg DBs when all reps hit |
| Calf Raises | 3 × 15 | BW+20 kg | +5 kg when all reps hit |

**Day C — Friday (Deadlift + Accessories)**

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Starting Weight | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deadlift | 3 × 3 | 145 kg | +5 kg when all reps hit for 2 consecutive weeks |
| Incline DB Press | 3 × 8 | 15 kg each | +2 kg when all reps hit |
| Single-Leg RDL | 3 × 8/leg | 16 kg DB | +2 kg when all reps hit |
| Plank | 3 × 30 sec | BW | Add 5 sec when it feels easy |

By Week 8, realistic targets: bench ~53–57 kg, squat holding at 105–115 kg, deadlift at 145–160 kg.

### Weeks 9–17: 2 days/week (Mon, Fri)

Volume drops but **you keep the weight on the bar.** Whatever you built to in Weeks 1–8, you hold here.

**Day A — Monday**

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Squat | 3 × 4 | Whatever you reached in Phase 1–2 |
| Bench Press | 3 × 5 | Keep progressing if possible (+1 kg/week), otherwise hold |
| Barbell Row | 3 × 6 | Hold weight from Phase 1–2 |
| Plank | 2 × 30–45 sec | BW |

**Day B — Friday**

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Deadlift | 2 × 3 | Hold weight from Phase 1–2 |
| Overhead Press | 3 × 6 | Hold or small progress |
| Pull-ups | 3 × 6 | BW or weighted — hold |
| Single-Leg RDL | 2 × 8/leg | Hold |

30–35 min sessions. The progression rule here: **if you can still add weight to bench, do it.** Everything else, just match your previous numbers. If you can't match them (legs are fried from running), drop 5–10% and don't worry about it.

### Weeks 18–20: 1 day or none

One light session in Week 18 at ~60% of your working weights, high reps (3×10). Just move and feel good. Weeks 19–20: no gym.

### Gym Rules

- Never go to failure. Stop 2–3 reps short. You need to recover for running.
- If your legs are toast from a long run, skip lower body. Not the end of the world.
- If you have to choose between gym and a run, choose the run (after Week 4).
- **Log your weights.** Even if it's just a note on your phone. You can't progressively overload what you don't track.

## Food

### The Simple Version

You need three things:

1. **~170g protein per day** (you're ~85 kg, so 2g per kg). This keeps your muscle.
2. **Eat a bit less than you burn** — roughly 400 kcal under maintenance. The running will do most of the work.
3. **Eat more carbs on hard/long days, fewer on rest days.** Don't overthink it — just have extra rice or pasta on the days you run hard.

That's it. If you do those three things, you will get leaner every week.

### What Your Day Looks Like

**Training Day (~2,800 kcal)**

| Meal | What | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 3 eggs + 2 toast + fruit | ~25g |
| Post-run / mid-morning | Protein shake + banana | ~30g |
| Lunch | Big portion of meat/fish (palm-and-a-half size) + rice/potato + vegetables | ~45g |
| Snack | Greek yogurt + handful of nuts | ~25g |
| Dinner | Meat/fish + carb source + vegetables | ~40g |
| Before bed | Cottage cheese or casein shake | ~25g |

**Rest Day (~2,200 kcal)**

Same meals but: skip the post-run shake, have smaller carb portions (half the rice/pasta/potato), add a bit more fat (avocado, olive oil, nuts). Protein stays the same.

### Food Rules

- **Protein at every meal.** Chicken, fish, beef, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, whey.
- **Carbs around running.** Oats, rice, potatoes, pasta, bread, bananas, fruit.
- **Don't fear fat.** Olive oil, avocado, nuts, eggs, salmon. Just don't go crazy.
- **Eat vegetables.** Every lunch and dinner. Non-negotiable for health and satiety.
- **Drink 3+ liters of water daily.** More on run days.
- **For runs over 60 min:** eat 1 gel or a few dates every 30–45 min. Practice this in training.

### What to Buy Weekly

**Proteins:** 1 kg chicken breast, 500g salmon or white fish, 500g lean beef or turkey, 2 dozen eggs, 1 kg Greek yogurt, 500g cottage cheese, whey protein.

**Carbs:** Oats, rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes, bread, bananas (6–8), mixed fruit/berries, pasta.

**Fats:** Olive oil, avocados (3–4), mixed nuts, peanut butter.

**Vegetables:** Broccoli, spinach, peppers, tomatoes, whatever you like and will actually eat.

### Supplements (only the ones that actually work)

| What | How much | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Creatine | 5g/day, every day | Preserves muscle in a deficit |
| Caffeine | Coffee before runs | Free speed |
| Vitamin D | 2000 IU/day | Most people are low, supports everything |
| Magnesium | 200–400mg before bed | Helps sleep and muscle recovery |

Skip everything else. Save your money.

### Tracking Progress

- Weigh yourself every morning, same conditions. Look at the **weekly average**, not daily swings.
- Target: **0.5–0.7 kg lost per week.** If it's faster, eat a bit more. If nothing's moving for 2 weeks, eat a bit less.
- Measure your waist weekly. More reliable than the scale.
- If your easy runs start feeling hard, or you're constantly tired, you're undereating. Bump calories up 200/day.

## Recovery (Keep It Short)

- **Sleep 8 hours.** This is the #1 thing. More important than any supplement or special food.
- **Stretch after every run.** 5 minutes: quads, calves, hip flexors, hamstrings. Done.
- **Foam roll when things feel tight.** Don't overthink it.
- **Replace running shoes every 600–800 km.**
- **If something hurts (sharp pain, not just soreness): stop, rest 3 days, reassess.** Don't run through injury.
- **Every 4th week is a recovery week** (already built into the plan). Don't skip these.
