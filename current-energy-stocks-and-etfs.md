## 1. Market Context: Why Energy is Running

The energy sector is in the middle of one of its strongest rallies in years, driven by a real supply shock rather than speculation.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| WTI Crude (start of 2026) | ~$60/bbl |
| WTI Crude (late March 2026) | ~$95-98/bbl |
| Brent Crude (late March 2026) | ~$110-118/bbl |
| YTD Oil Price Surge | ~70% |
| XLE Energy ETF (YTD) | +37.9% |
| S&P 500 (YTD) | -4.4% |

**What's driving it:** The Strait of Hormuz -- which handles ~20% of global oil supply -- has been effectively closed to most shipping traffic since late February 2026 following U.S.-Iran military escalation. Insurance companies cancelled war-risk coverage for Persian Gulf vessels, creating an 8-11 million barrel/day supply deficit. This is being called "the largest supply shock in market history" by Goldman Sachs.

## 2. Oil Price Outlook: How Long Does This Last?

Understanding the price trajectory is critical for timing entries and exits.

### Goldman Sachs Forecasts (March 2026)

| Period | Brent Forecast | WTI Forecast |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| March 2026 | ~$110-120/bbl | ~$98/bbl |
| April 2026 | ~$115/bbl | ~$105/bbl |
| Q2 2026 avg | $76/bbl | $71/bbl |
| 2026 full-year avg | $85/bbl | ~$80/bbl |
| Q4 2026 (base case) | $71/bbl | ~$66/bbl |
| Q4 2026 (extended disruption) | $93-100/bbl | ~$88-95/bbl |

### EIA Forecast

- Brent Q2 2026 average: ~$91/bbl (higher than Goldman's base case)
- Production shut-ins expected to peak in early April, then gradually ease

### What This Means for Investors

- **Near-term (April-May):** Prices likely stay elevated. Peak uncertainty = peak energy stock premiums.
- **Mid-term (Q2-Q3):** If Hormuz reopens as expected, oil retreats to $70s-$80s. Energy stocks will give back some gains.
- **Extended scenario:** If disruptions persist 2+ additional months, oil stays $90-100+ and energy stocks continue rallying.

**The trade window is now through early summer.** After that, the direction depends entirely on conflict resolution.

## 5. Refinery Stocks: The Hidden Winners

## 9. Leveraged ETFs: Maximum Short-Term Exposure

Leveraged ETFs provide amplified daily returns and are designed for short-term trading, not buy-and-hold.

| ETF | Leverage | YTD Return | Expense Ratio | Assets |
|-----|----------|------------|---------------|--------|
| **GUSH** | 2x Oil & Gas E&P | +117% | 0.94% | $296M |
| **ERX** | 2x Energy Sector | +53.6% | 0.91% | $315M |
| **WTIU** | 3x Energy | Volatile | ~1% | Smaller |

### How They Work

- GUSH returns 2x the daily return of the S&P Oil & Gas E&P Select Industry Index
- ERX returns 2x the daily return of the Energy Select Sector Index
- Daily compounding means these ETFs decay over time -- a 10% drop followed by a 10% gain nets negative, not flat

### When to Use

- Hold for **days to weeks**, not months
- Use when you have high conviction on a directional move in oil prices
- Set strict stop-losses (10-15% trailing stops are common)
- GUSH for more aggressive E&P exposure, ERX for broader energy sector

### When to Avoid

- Choppy, sideways markets (decay eats returns)
- As a long-term hold (compounding decay guarantees underperformance)
- If you can't monitor positions daily

## 12. Key Risks & Exit Signals

### Exit Signal #1: Ceasefire or Hormuz Reopening

The single biggest risk to all these positions. Monitor:
- U.S.-Iran diplomatic channels
- Insurance companies reinstating Gulf coverage
- Tanker traffic resuming through the Strait

**Impact:** Oil drops $20-30/bbl within days. Tanker stocks fall 30-50%. E&P and majors pull back 10-20%. Refiners are most resilient (margins can persist).

### Exit Signal #2: OPEC+ Emergency Production Increase

If Saudi Arabia, UAE, and other producers with spare capacity ramp output to fill the gap.

**Impact:** Oil drops $10-15/bbl. Upstream stocks soften. Services stocks decline on reduced drilling urgency.

### Exit Signal #3: Global Demand Destruction

If prices stay elevated long enough to trigger recession-level demand destruction.

**Impact:** Oil drops sharply as demand evaporates. All energy stocks decline. Defensive sectors (staples, healthcare, utilities) outperform.
