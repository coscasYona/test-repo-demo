# Nutrition Plan: Getting Shredded While Training for a Marathon

## The Balancing Act

Getting shredded while training for a marathon is a realistic but nuanced goal. You need to:

- **Eat enough** to fuel your training and recover
- **Eat little enough** to lose body fat
- **Eat enough protein** to preserve your muscle mass
- **Time your carbs** around hard training sessions

The good news: as your weekly running volume climbs from ~16 km to ~65 km, your total energy expenditure will skyrocket. You won't need an aggressive deficit — the training itself creates much of the caloric gap.

## Estimated Caloric Needs

Assuming body weight of ~85 kg (adjust if different):

| Phase | Estimated TDEE | Target Intake | Deficit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1–2 (Weeks 1–8) | ~2,800–3,000 kcal | ~2,400–2,600 kcal | ~400 kcal/day |
| Phase 3 (Weeks 9–12) | ~3,100–3,300 kcal | ~2,700–2,900 kcal | ~400 kcal/day |
| Phase 4 (Weeks 13–17) | ~3,400–3,600 kcal | ~3,000–3,200 kcal | ~400 kcal/day |
| Phase 5 (Weeks 18–20) | ~2,800–3,000 kcal | ~2,800–3,000 kcal | **None — eat at maintenance** |

**Key:** The deficit stays moderate (~400 kcal/day) throughout. As training volume increases, total food intake also increases. During the taper, eat at maintenance — you want to be fully fueled for race day.

## Macro Targets

### Protein: 2.0 g/kg/day = ~170g

This is non-negotiable. Research consistently shows this level preserves lean mass during energy restriction in athletes (Morton et al., 2018; Mettler et al., 2010).

- Distribute across 4–5 meals (~35–42g per meal)
- Include a pre-sleep protein source (casein, cottage cheese, Greek yogurt)
- Prioritize complete protein sources (animal, dairy, soy, or complementary plant proteins)

### Carbohydrates: Periodized by Day

| Day Type | Carbs (g/kg) | Carbs (total ~85 kg) | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard/Long Run Day | 5–6 g/kg | 425–510g | Long run day, interval day |
| Easy Run + Gym Day | 3–4 g/kg | 255–340g | Easy run + lifting day |
| Rest Day | 2–3 g/kg | 170–255g | Full rest, recovery only |

This is the **carbohydrate periodization** approach (Impey et al., 2018). You fuel for the work required — high carbs when you need performance, lower carbs when you don't.

### Fat: 0.8–1.0 g/kg/day = ~68–85g

Fats support hormone production (testosterone, cortisol regulation), joint health, and satiety. Never go below 0.7 g/kg — especially in a deficit.

## Nutrient Timing

### Pre-Run (60–90 min before)

- 30–50g easily digestible carbs + small protein
- Examples: banana + handful of almonds, toast with honey, small oatmeal bowl
- Avoid high fiber/fat — it slows digestion and can cause GI distress

### During Run (for runs > 60 min)

- Start fueling at 45–60 min mark
- 30–60g carbs per hour (energy gels, sports drink, dates)
- **Practice this in training** — never try something new on race day

### Post-Run (within 60 min)

- 30–40g protein + 50–80g carbs
- Examples: protein shake + banana, chicken + rice, Greek yogurt + fruit + granola
- This window matters most after hard sessions and long runs

### Pre-Sleep

- 30–40g slow-digesting protein
- Cottage cheese, casein shake, or Greek yogurt
- Research (Snijders et al., 2015) shows this improves overnight muscle protein synthesis and recovery

## Hydration

| Context | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Daily baseline | ~35 ml per kg body weight = ~3 liters |
| During runs | 150–250 ml every 15–20 min |
| Post-run | Replenish 150% of fluid lost (weigh before/after) |
| Electrolytes | Add sodium/electrolytes for runs > 60 min and on hot days |

## Supplements (Evidence-Based Only)

| Supplement | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Creatine Monohydrate | 5g/day | Preserves lean mass in deficit, aids recovery (Kreider et al., 2017) |
| Caffeine | 3–6 mg/kg pre-run | Proven ergogenic aid for endurance (Goldstein et al., 2010) |
| Vitamin D | 2,000–4,000 IU/day | Most people deficient; supports bone health and immune function |
| Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) | 2–3g/day | Anti-inflammatory, supports joint health |
| Magnesium | 200–400 mg/day | Supports sleep, muscle function; often depleted in athletes |
| Electrolyte mix | During long runs | Sodium, potassium, magnesium for hydration |

**Skip:** Fat burners, BCAAs (redundant with adequate protein), testosterone boosters, and anything with a proprietary blend.

## Tracking Progress

Weigh yourself daily, first thing in the morning, after using the bathroom. Use a **7-day rolling average** — daily fluctuations are meaningless.

| Target Rate of Loss | Notes |
|---|---|
| 0.5–0.7 kg/week | Ideal range for preserving muscle while losing fat |
| > 1.0 kg/week | Too aggressive — increase calories by 200 |
| 0 kg/week for 2+ weeks | Plateau — decrease calories by 150 or add 1 easy run |

Also track:
- **Waist measurement** (weekly) — more reliable than scale for body comp
- **Run performance** — if easy pace is getting harder, you may be underfueling
- **Sleep quality** — poor sleep = poor recovery = poor results
- **Mood and energy** — chronic fatigue or irritability means the deficit is too aggressive

## Phase 5 Special: Race Week Carb Loading

In the final 2–3 days before the marathon:

- Increase carbs to **8–10 g/kg/day** (680–850g)
- Reduce fat and fiber to make room
- Reduce protein slightly (~1.6 g/kg is fine for 2–3 days)
- This maximizes glycogen storage — the research shows it can improve marathon performance by 2–3% (Hawley et al., 1997)

**This is NOT "eat everything in sight."** It's strategically increasing carbs while keeping total calories moderate. Think: extra rice, pasta, bread, potatoes, sports drinks — not pizza and donuts.
